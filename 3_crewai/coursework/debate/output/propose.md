I strongly support the motion that there should be strict laws to regulate LLMs.

LLMs are not harmless tools anymore; they are powerful systems that can shape what people believe, how they work, and even how they make decisions. Without strict regulation, we are essentially allowing a few companies to deploy technologies that can spread misinformation, enable fraud, invade privacy, and reinforce bias at massive scale with little accountability.

First, the risk of harm is too large to leave to self-regulation. LLMs can generate convincing fake news, impersonate real people, and automate scams. A single model can be used millions of times, meaning one flaw can cause widespread damage quickly. Strict laws are necessary to require safety testing, transparency, and clear responsibility when harm occurs.

Second, LLMs influence critical areas like education, healthcare, hiring, and law. In these contexts, errors are not minor—they can ruin lives. If a model gives biased, inaccurate, or discriminatory outputs, there must be legal standards to prevent that. Just as we regulate medicine, finance, and aviation because mistakes are costly, we should regulate LLMs because their mistakes can be equally serious.

Third, without strict laws, power becomes concentrated in the hands of a few corporations. They decide what data is used, what biases are embedded, and who gets access. Regulation protects the public interest, ensures transparency, and stops companies from putting profit ahead of safety.

The choice is not between innovation and regulation. It is between responsible innovation and dangerous chaos. Strict laws do not stop progress—they make progress trustworthy. If LLMs are going to shape our future, then they must be governed by strong rules, clear accountability, and public oversight.
